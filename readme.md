# [DOA Estimation] Co-array tensor completion for direction of arrival estimation [with MATLAB code]

The tensor-based sparse array direction of arrival (DOA) estimation technique has been developed for processing multidimensional Nyquist sampled signals. Additionally, co-array tensor processing that matches the Nyquist sampling can be achieved through the derivation of an enlarged virtual array. However, some of the co-augmented sparse arrays correspond to discontinuous virtual arrays, and existing methods can only utilize their continuous parts; traditional virtual linear array interpolation techniques can complete the co-array covariance matrix with discrete missing elements, but they cannot complete the co-array tensor with full layers of missing data.
This paper proposes a co-array tensor completion algorithm for two-dimensional DOA estimation, which can fully utilize the statistical information of the co-array tensor. Specifically, to impose effective low-rank regularization on the co-array tensor with layer missingness, this paper introduces displacement dimension enlargement and co-array tensor reshaping methods, reconstructing it into a structured co-array tensor with sufficient discrete missing elements; simultaneously, by maximizing the ratio of the discreteness - proportion of the missing elements, the shape of the reconstructed co-array tensor is optimized. On this basis, a co-array tensor nuclear norm minimization problem can be constructed, optimizing the co-array tensor corresponding to the completed virtual array, thereby achieving closed-form estimation of DOA. Theoretical proofs demonstrate the global convergence of this co-array tensor completion method, and simulation results show that compared to other matrix-based and tensor-based methods, the proposed algorithm is effective.
Introduction: Wave Direction of Arrival (DOA) Estimation Based on Arrays has been widely studied in fields such as radar, sonar, speech, and wireless communication. Unlike uniform arrays, sparse arrays (including interleaved arrays, nested arrays, and other optimized configurations) can estimate the wave direction of signals at sub-Nyquist sampling rates, which helps to mitigate antenna coupling effects and system overload issues. Typically, the second order signal statistics of sparse arrays can be derived into an extended virtual array for co-array processing matching with Nyquist sampling. Common methods for DOA estimation based on co-arrays include Covariance Matrix Inversion (CMI), MUSIC, and ESPRIT, among others. These methods follow matrix-based signal processing principles, which involve first representing the received signal as a matrix and then expanding its second order co-array statistics into vectors. However, as the dimensionality of sparse arrays increases, these methods struggle to preserve the structural characteristics of multidimensional Nyquist signals.
As an extension of matrices in high-dimensional space, tensors have been used for multi-dimensional array processing. For example, tensor decomposition techniques have been proposed for estimating potential multi-dimensional parameters in wireless communication and radar systems. Specifically, methods such as the Van Dam structure constrained tensor decomposition and the Hermitian Toeplitz constrained tensor reconstruction have been developed for two-dimensional DOA estimation in transmit beamforming MIMO radar and coherent source DOA estimation in uniform rectangular arrays, respectively. By combining tensor models with sub-Nyquist sampling, researchers have proposed a sparse array tensor sub-Nyquist radar for DOA estimation, which recovers one-dimensional sub-Nyquist tensor signals through tensor compression sensing. In recent years, to achieve co-array tensor DOA estimation, researchers have derived an extended multi-dimensional virtual array by extending the sub-Nyquist tensor model to the co-array domain.
However, for certain augmentable sparse arrays (such as typical interleaved arrays), the corresponding multi-dimensional discontinuous virtual arrays exhibit "hole layers," leading to complete layer element omission in the co-array tensor. Existing methods for direction of arrival (DOA) estimation in co-arrays for such augmentable sparse arrays must discard discontinuous virtual sensors to extract a virtual uniform array. This results in the statistical information of the co-array being underutilized, and the tensor processing techniques struggle to compensate for the reduction in aperture size and resolution issues caused by this. Therefore, fully utilizing the derived virtual arrays to enhance estimation performance has become an urgent issue to be addressed.
To fully leverage all the information in the co-array, researchers have proposed virtual array interpolation techniques for wave direction estimation in interleaved linear arrays, with matrix completion and matrix reconstruction being two mainstream solutions. Specifically, matrix completion restores random missing elements in the damaged data matrix through low-rank regularization (usually relaxed to minimize the nuclear norm), targeting the incomplete co-array covariance matrix corresponding to the discontinuous virtual linear array. Research has already proposed optimization methods for minimizing the nuclear norm to obtain the interpolated virtual linear array; on the other hand, to achieve unstructured wave direction estimation, atoms norm minimization is used to reconstruct the complete co-array covariance matrix corresponding to the interpolated virtual linear array, with the Toeplitz property serving as prior information. However, these virtual linear array interpolation techniques are only applicable to incomplete matrices with discrete missing elements and cannot fill in the complete co-array tensors that contain complete layer omissions.
Due to the strong correlation between adjacent missing elements in incomplete tensors, researchers have proposed a tensor completion technique based on low-rank regularization to recover random missing elements. This technique has been applied to repair image data affected by random pixel missingness. However, complete layer missingness can lead to difficulties in applying adequate low-rank regularization to coarray tensors. For rule-damaged images with whole-layer pixel missingness, methods based on embedded space layer completion have received widespread attention. These methods approximate the Tucker representation or Tensor Train representation of an incomplete image tensor through iterative rank increment processes. However, both the Tucker rank and Tensor Train rank increment operations are not compatible with the paradigmatic polynomial (CP) model of coarray tensors, meaning that existing image restoration layer completion methods cannot be applied to coarray tensor completion.
In recent years, researchers have proposed a tensor completion method based on CP approximation for reconstructing functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data with a regular sub-Nyquist sampling pattern (including fiber missing and layer missing). However, this method modifies observational elements in the incomplete tensor, while observational elements in the waveform direction estimation require preservation in coarray tensors. Therefore, how to effectively complete the coarray tensor with layer missingness for waveform direction estimation remains a challenging task, and relevant research has yet to address this issue.
This paper proposes a two-dimensional sparse array wave direction estimation algorithm based on co-array tensor completion. Firstly, incomplete co-array tensors corresponding to the extended discontinuous virtual cubic array are derived from sub-Nyquist tensor statistics. Due to the presence of complete layers missing in co-array tensors, traditional low-rank tensor completion techniques struggle to be effective. To address this issue, by employing displacement dimension expansion and co-array tensor reshaping, the initial co-array tensor is reconstructed into a structured tensor with discrete missing elements; meanwhile, by maximizing the degree of discreteness of the missing elements and minimizing their proportion, the shape of the reconstructed co-array tensor is optimized. On this basis, the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) is employed to solve the problem of minimizing the nuclear norm of the co-array tensor, successfully completing the reconstruction of the co-array tensor and theoretically proving the global convergence of this co-array tensor completion method. Finally, the full co-array tensor corresponding to the completed virtual cubic array is decomposed, yielding closed-form solutions for azimuth and elevation angles. Numerical simulation results show that the proposed co-array tensor completion algorithm outperforms traditional methods based on matrices and tensors in terms of estimation accuracy and angular resolution, and can handle both non-coherent signals and completely coherent signals simultaneously.
In previous research on sparse array beamforming direction estimation, only the continuous parts of multi-dimensional discontinuous virtual arrays were extracted for co-array tensor processing, without exploring the issue of co-array tensor completion with layer missingness. In studies on coherent source beamforming direction estimation in multipath environments, although a tensor de-correlation method was proposed for uniform arrays, the sub-Nyquist sampling scenarios were not considered. In contrast, this paper validates the effectiveness of co-array tensor processing in sparse array coherent source estimation. The preliminary results of this paper have been presented in conference papers. Building on this, through designing an optimization problem for the size of co-array sub-tensors, further research was conducted on the optimal structure of reconstructed co-array tensors. A detailed derivation of the co-array tensor completion problem was provided, and theoretical analyses were made on the discrete degree of elements in the reconstructed co-array tensor, the global convergence of co-array tensor completion, and computational complexity.
The rest of this article is structured as follows: Section 2 introduces the tensor signal model for sparse arrays; Section 3 discusses the reconstruction of incomplete co-array tensors to achieve a scattered distribution of missing elements; Section 4 proposes a co-array tensor completion method for direction of arrival (DOA) estimation; Section 5 discusses simulation results; and Section 6 summarizes the paper.
Conclusion: This paper presents a co-array tensor completion algorithm for two-dimensional DOA estimation. The algorithm fully leverages the discontinuous virtual array derived from the mutual correlation tensor statistics, representing the corresponding equivalent second-order signal with an incomplete co-array tensor. To impose effective low-rank regularization on the co-array tensor, the principle of maximizing the discreteness - proportion ratio (DPR) of missing elements is applied. This leads to a thorough scattering treatment of the entire layer of missing elements in the co-array tensor. On this basis, the co-array tensor nuclear norm minimization problem is solved using the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM), achieving the completion of the co-array tensor reconstruction while theoretically proving the global convergence of the co-array tensor completion problem. Subsequently, the complete co-array tensor corresponding to the completed virtual array is subjected to Canonical Polynomial Decomposition (CPD), which estimates the two-dimensional DOA in closed form. Simulation results confirm that compared to similar methods, the proposed algorithm exhibits superior performance in terms of estimation accuracy and resolution.
The proposed algorithm can be generalized to other parameter estimation scenarios based on partially augmented sparse arrays. For example, methods for completing incomplete co-array tensors, which are applicable to sparse MIMO systems, can utilize multidimensional co-array statistical information to achieve angle-frequency joint estimation while maximizing multidimensional co-array statistics; furthermore, in imaging scenarios, the designed tensor segmentation and structured tensor construction methods can be used for image restoration or acceleration in imaging processes.
